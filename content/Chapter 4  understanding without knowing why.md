
Both Alex and Maria lay in their separate beds, the house silent in a way that felt heavier than noise. Neither of them slept.
Every hour stretched between them, filled with the things they had said—and the things they hadn’t been able to.
Alex stared at the ceiling, his chest tight, replaying every word like a punishment he couldn’t turn off. Maria turned onto her side, eyes open in the dark, wondering how someone could feel so close and so far away at the same time.
And somewhere in the middle of that silence, they both realised the same thing: nothing between them felt simple anymore.

Alex lay rigid in the dark, eyes fixed on nothing, thoughts looping tighter with every second.
“I love her,” he whispered under his breath, barely audible to the empty room. “I’m in love with her…”
His throat tightened. He dragged a hand over his face, like he could physically push the thoughts away.
“…and I’m mad at myself for hiding the truth.”
Silence answered him.
His jaw clenched as his breathing grew uneven. “I need to tell her,” he murmured, quieter now, almost broken. “I need to… but I don’t even know why I can’t.”
And in that question, everything he was afraid of sat waiting in the dark with him.
A sudden crash shattered the silence.

Maria jolted upright in bed.
It sounded like glass—something breaking cleanly, violently, like it had been thrown with too much force to take back. Her heart jumped as she threw on something and ran down the hallway. ''Alex?” she called, breath uneven.
When she pushed his door open, the sight stopped her.
He was standing there, chest rising and falling fast, a broken plate scattered across the floor at his feet. His hand was still half-raised like he hadn’t realised he’d done it until it was already too late.
For a second, neither of them spoke. Then his voice cracked.
“I can’t do this,” he said, shaking his head. “I can’t keep holding it in.”
Maria stepped closer carefully. “Alex…”
His eyes flicked to hers—wild, exhausted, like something inside him had finally given way.
“When I was sixteen…” he started, then stopped, swallowing hard. His hands trembled at his sides. “A man used to come around my mother’s place. I called him my uncle. I trusted him.”
His jaw tightened like saying it hurt.
“I didn’t understand it back then. I just knew something about it wasn’t right. But I didn’t know how to stop it. I didn’t know how to tell anyone.”
Maria’s breath caught, but she didn’t interrupt. She just stood there, listening as the weight of it unfolded.
Alex looked down at the broken plate like he couldn’t stand to look at anything else.
“That’s why I don’t know how to be normal with you,” he said quietly. “That’s why I control things. That’s why I pull away. Because I know what it feels like when I don’t have control over my own life.”
His voice broke at the end.

“I’m trying,” he whispered. “But I don’t know how to stop being like this.”
The room went still except for their breathing—and the sound of something finally being said out loud.
His breath hitched as the words left him, and then it all collapsed at once. The anger drained out of his face, replaced by something raw and unbearable.
He started crying—quiet at first, like he was trying to fight it, then harder, until he had to look away.
Maria didn’t speak. She just crossed the room slowly, careful not to scare him, and lowered herself beside him.
“Sit down,” she said softly, steadying him by the arm.
He let her. Almost like he didn’t have the strength not to.
Alex sank onto the edge of the bed, elbows on his knees, head bowed as he tried to breathe through it. Maria stayed close, not touching too much, just enough so he knew she was there.
And for the first time that night, the silence between them wasn’t empty—it was holding him together instead of pulling them apart.

“I don’t know who I am anymore,” he said, voice breaking as he stared down at his hands. “No one ever knew it happened… just me.”
His breath shook, uneven, like he couldn’t get enough air in.
“My mum never believed me,” he added quietly, and that was what finally cracked something open in him completely.
Maria didn’t interrupt. She just stayed beside him, steady, present, letting the words exist without trying to fix them.
Alex wiped his face quickly, almost angrily, like he was ashamed of crying. But his voice came out smaller now, exhausted.
“I’ve been carrying it alone for so long… I don’t even know where I end and it starts anymore.”
“I’m here for you,” Maria said softly.
She didn’t hesitate this time—she moved closer and wrapped her arms around him, steady and warm, grounding him when he felt like he was falling apart.
“I can help you,” she added quietly, her voice close to his ear. “You don’t have to carry it alone anymore.”
Alex didn’t answer at first. He just stayed there, rigid for a moment, like he didn’t know how to accept it. Then slowly, something in him broke down again—but softer this time. He leaned into her, like he’d finally run out of strength to hold himself up.
“Can I stay with you tonight?” Maria asked quietly, still holding him. “You shouldn’t be alone right now.”
She pulled back just enough to look at him. “We’ll get you help. Therapy, maybe. Something real. But it’s late… tomorrow we’ll figure it out properly. We’ll search for what you need.”
Alex didn’t answer straight away. His eyes were red, unfocused, like he was trying to process what “help” even meant without it feeling like another thing he might lose control over.
Finally, he gave a small, shaky nod.
“Okay,” he whispered.

And for the first time that night, he didn’t try to push her away For the first time in a long while, I felt something that almost resembled hope.
We were in the same bed, the silence no longer sharp or empty, just… tired. Alex lay beside me, still too tense to fully relax, but no longer alone in it either. Now I knew. What he had carried. What he had buried for ten years without anyone truly seeing it.
I looked at him in the dark and felt my chest ache for him in a different way than before—not confusion anymore, but understanding. He didn’t say much. He didn’t have to.
And as I lay there beside him, I realised this wasn’t fixed, not even close—but for the first time, we were finally in the same place, at the same time, holding the same truth between us.

He shifted slowly, like he was still unsure he was allowed to. Then, carefully, he laid his head against my chest.
For a moment, neither of us spoke. I could feel his breathing—uneven at first, then gradually slowing, like his body was finally starting to believe he was safe.
My arms came around him instinctively, holding him without pressure, just presence.
And something in the room changed. Not dramatically. Not like a miracle. Just quietly—like a knot loosening after being tight for too long. He didn’t say anything. He didn’t need to.
For the first time in what felt like forever, he wasn’t fighting to hold himself together. 

The next morning felt different, quieter in a way that wasn’t heavy anymore. Just still.
We had breakfast together, something simple, but even that felt new after everything from the night before. Alex was more withdrawn than usual, careful in the way he moved and spoke, like he was still unsure whether last night had been real or just something he’d broken open in himself by accident.
We talked about it anyway. Not everything, not all at once—but enough. Enough to not pretend it hadn’t happened.
“I don’t really know how to do this,” he admitted at one point, staring at his mug instead of me.
“I know,” I said softly. “But you don’t have to do it alone anymore.”
That seemed to sit with him.
After breakfast, I opened my laptop and we sat side by side, searching properly this time. Real help. Real names. Not just thoughts in the dark. We scrolled for nearly an hour before we found her—**Dr Emily Heart**.
Her description was calm, grounded, the kind that didn’t promise to fix everything, just to help you understand it. And for the first time, that felt like enough.
Alex didn’t say much when we stopped on her profile, but I noticed the way his shoulders loosened slightly, like even the idea of someone else holding some of the weight made breathing easier.

We found her number and called.
My hand shook slightly as I held the phone on speaker, Alex sitting beside me, quiet in a way that felt tense rather than distant.
We explained the situation briefly—carefully, not going too deep, just enough for them to understand that this wasn’t something small or recent.
The voice on the other end stayed calm the entire time. Professional. Steady. It helped more than I expected. By the end of the call, the appointment was set for tomorrow at 12.
When I hung up, neither of us spoke for a moment.
Alex exhaled slowly. “I want you there,” he said quietly, not looking at me. “For support.”
I nodded immediately. “I’ll be there.” And for the first time since everything started to unravel, the next step didn’t feel like falling—it felt like moving forward.

I reached for him without thinking and pulled him into a hug.
“It’s going to be okay,” I said quietly, holding him close. “We’re going to get through this.”
We stayed like that for a few seconds longer than either of us probably expected—just breathing, just existing in the same moment without fear or distance between us.
When I pulled back, I realised my eyes were wet too. I hadn’t even noticed the tears until that point. Alex looked at me like he wanted to believe me. Like he was trying to hold onto the idea that things could actually change this time.

This shift between us—this quiet change—finally made it feel like something good might be coming our way.
Not perfect. Not magically healed. But different. Softer. More honest.
For so long, it had felt like we were standing on opposite sides of a wall neither of us knew how to break down. And now, for the first time, there was a crack in it. A way through.
I looked at Alex and realised I wasn’t searching for the version of us we used to be anymore.
Maybe this—this raw, painful, real version—was the beginning of something better.
This was bringing us closer to each other in ways I’d wanted for years without even realising it.

For the first time, I had him beside me at night instead of behind a closed door. And for the first time, he let himself have me there during the worst moment of his life—not as control, not as distance, but as comfort.
There was something painfully intimate about it. The way his arm stayed loosely around my waist even in sleep, like he was afraid I might disappear if he let go. The way he breathed easier whenever I touched him.
I knew healing wouldn’t happen overnight. Some mornings would still be hard. Some memories would still follow him into the dark. But this no longer felt like two strangers trying to force a relationship to survive.
It felt like we were finally learning each other for real.

I didn’t know what the future would bring for us anymore. I didn’t know how long healing would take, or how many bad days still waited ahead.
But for the first time in a long time, I wasn’t scared of the future.
Because whatever came next, we were finally facing it together instead of alone.
And somehow, that felt like enough.
I was here for all of it—the difficult parts, the quiet parts, the rebuilding parts. Him. Us. Whatever this became.
